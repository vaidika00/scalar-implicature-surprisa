This project tests whether GPT-2 surprisal at scalar-implicature trigger words ("some", "or") corresponds to the processing-cost patterns observed in human reading times, using experimental materials from Breheny, Katsos & Williams (2006).

Repository Contents
surprisal_pipeline_v2.py - main pipeline. Loads GPT-2 small and GPT-2-medium, computes word-level surprisal at the critical trigger word for each of the 8 experimental items, runs paired statistical comparisons (paired t-test, Wilcoxon signed-rank), and compares results against the human reading-time benchmarks reported in the source paper.
surprisal_results_both_models.csv - output of the pipeline: per-item, per-model surprisal values for all 8 items × 2 conditions × 2 models.
README.md - this file.

Data Source

Experimental items (Appendix A.1 and A.3) and human reading-time benchmarks (Table 5, and main text) are taken from:

Breheny, R., Katsos, N., & Williams, J. (2006). Are generalised scalar implicatures generated by default? An on-line investigation into the role of context in generating pragmatic inferences. Cognition, 100(3), 434–463. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2005.07.003

Method Summary
8 items: 4 pairs with trigger word "some", 4 pairs with trigger word "or"
Each pair: an upper-bound context (implicature pragmatically supported) and a lower-bound context (implicature not supported)
Surprisal computed at the critical trigger word only (not whole-sentence), matching the segment-level reading-time measure used in the original human study
Two models (GPT-2 small, GPT-2-medium) used as a robustness check across model scale
